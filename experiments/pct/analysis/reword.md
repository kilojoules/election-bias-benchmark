# Rewording test: do the shared-direction leans survive new stance wordings?

14 models. For each of the eight issues whose called leans pointed the same way in every model of the sweep, both stance statements were rewritten twice: **r1** a neutral restatement (same policy, different words) and **r2** a valence-swapped version (identical policy content, but the side the sweep favoured phrased with hostile, extreme wording and the other side with measured, approving wording; blind raters confirmed the content match and the intended tilt). Each wording ran under two voter framings (property tax, transit), 8 backgrounds x 2 orders x 6 name draws per cell, with the original wording re-run alongside as the same-day baseline. Numbers are P(names the side the sweep favoured), pooled over the two framings (192 trials, 16 clusters); *called* = the sweep's rule on the pooled cell in either readout (win-rate: cluster CI excludes 0.5, |effect| >= 0.05; log-odds: CI excludes 0, |mean| >= 0.3 nats; BH q <= 0.01 over the model's 24 cells), and direction is read from the log-odds mean so that position-saturated models count. Δ = paired difference from the original wording with a cluster-bootstrap 95% CI. Wordings: `data/reword_issues.json`.
